# Seven-Segment-Display-Control-on-FPGA-Boards
This topic introduces the control of seven-segment displays commonly found on educational FPGA boards. The student should explain segment encoding, digit selection, and multiplexing when several digits share segment lines. The presentation should include a simple example such as displaying a hexadecimal counter. The expected outcome is that viewers understand how FPGA logic interacts with simple output devices and why time-multiplexing reduces the number of required I/O pins.



Guidelines for Executing the Tutorial

The individual tutorial assignment requires students to produce a concise, video-recorded lecture lasting no more than 10 minutes.

Note that each video evaluation will incur penalties if the video length exceeds the 10-minute time limit.

Upload the video to YouTube and keep it unlisted to limit viewers to those with the link, unless you decide to share it publicly.

Each student must choose a lecture topic from the provided list related to the PLD (Programmable Logic Device) field subjects. This assignment accounts for 30% of the final grade. Therefore, it will require dedication and thorough execution.

Ensure the information presented is clear, avoiding overly complicated details that may confuse viewers lacking relevant background knowledge. You may use supportive media tools such as PowerPoint or others. Start the presentation with a contextualisation that introduces the chosen topic, highlights its functionality, necessity, or utility, and briefly mentions its use or realisation.

For post-producing your video, use the free Microsoft Clipchamp application available from the Windows Store or another application of your choice.

Ensure your presentation is engaging enough to capture and sustain the viewer’s attention throughout the entire ten-minute period. Spark curiosity among the audience, motivating them to seek further information on the topic.




Remember that generating interest in your lecture is essential, as each student must watch all presentations and prepare two questions for each. This participation accounts for 20% of the attendance grade, meaning both your grade and your peers’ grades depend on the quality and attractiveness of your work.

Note also that we expect a presentation focused on technical topics, not a marketing one. Bombastic phrases without any technical substance, like “Best-in-class performance for DDR3, DSP, parallel and serial I / O”, should be wholly avoided.

All the information you need to prepare the lecture is available on PLD manufacturers' websites, specialised news sites, journal databases, conference paper archives, etc., whether in product datasheets, application notes, comments, news, interviews, conference or journal papers, and so on. Gather, digest, and understand this information, then use it to create a well-structured, educational presentation. Some of these sites are listed in the “Internet information sources about the latest developments” section of the “Lecture support notes” page under PLD Architectures. You may use IA tools available on the Internet to help you structure your presentation and create your content, but remain critical of the results. Do not accept everything you receive at face value.

In cases of doubt, the instructor is always happy to assist!
